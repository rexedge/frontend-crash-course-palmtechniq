# Assignment 09 — Real Content Pass

**Type:** Stretch · **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ · **Time:** ~1.5 hr
**Prereqs:** a finished portfolio ([Assignment 01](01-personal-portfolio.md))
**Build in:** `work/01-personal-portfolio/` — make a save point first

---

## Goal

Replace everything on your portfolio that is a stand-in — placeholder pictures, vague sentences, filler — with the real thing. No new code: this assignment is about words and images, which is what visitors actually come for.

## What you'll practice

- Writing about your own work clearly and honestly
- Taking and preparing screenshots
- Writing `alt` text that does its job
- Reading your own page as a stranger would

## Instructions

1. **Find every stand-in.** Search all your files for `picsum`, `Lorem`, `Placeholder`, `example.com`, and any sentence you copied from the lesson. Make a list.
2. **Take a screenshot of each project.** Open the project, size the window so the page looks its best, and capture it. Save each one in `images/` with a short, lower-case name and no spaces: `gallery.png`, not `Screenshot (14).png`.
3. **Use a real photo of yourself** — or, if you'd rather not, a picture that stands for you. It must be one you took, or one you have permission to use.
4. **Rewrite each project as a small case study**, in three short parts:
   - **The problem** — what was the page for?
   - **What I built** — and with what. Be specific: "a 12-photo gallery using `auto-fit` and one media query", not "a nice gallery".
   - **What I learned** — one real thing, including what went wrong.
5. **Rewrite your pitch and your bio.** Short sentences. Say what you can do, and what you're looking for. Cut every word that could describe anybody: "passionate", "hard-working", "detail-oriented".
6. **Rewrite every `alt`** to describe what the picture now shows.
7. **Point the links at real places**: your own profiles, not the home pages of those sites. Remove any link you don't have a real destination for.
8. **Read the whole page out loud.** Fix every sentence you stumble over.

## Requirements

- [ ] No placeholder pictures, and no placeholder text, anywhere
- [ ] Every image is your own, or one you have permission to use
- [ ] Each project has all three parts: the problem, what you built, what you learned
- [ ] Every `alt` describes the real picture
- [ ] Every link goes to a real, relevant page
- [ ] Nothing on the page is untrue — you're a beginner with eight classes of work to show, and that's a perfectly good thing to be
- [ ] The page still works at every width: longer text hasn't broken the cards

## Acceptance criteria

- Searching your files for `picsum`, `Lorem`, and `Placeholder` finds nothing.
- Someone who has never met you can say, after one read, what you can build and what you'd like to do next.
- Every claim on the page is something you could demonstrate if asked.
- The project cards still line up, even though the descriptions are now different lengths.

## Hints

<details><summary>Show hint</summary>

- **Screenshots:** `Windows + Shift + S` on Windows, `Cmd + Shift + 4` on a Mac. Capture the page, not your whole desktop.
- **A good `alt` for a screenshot** says what the page is, not that it's a screenshot of your work: `A photo gallery with twelve square photos in four columns` beats `Screenshot of my project`.
- **Stuck on "what I learned"?** Look in your wrap-up reflection, or think about the bug that took longest. The thing that went wrong is usually the most interesting thing you can say.
- **A long project write-up doesn't fit on a card.** Keep one sentence on the card, and put the rest behind a `More details` button — the recipe is in [Assignment 01](01-personal-portfolio.md) — or on a page of its own ([Assignment 08](08-multi-page.md)).
</details>

## Stretch (optional)

Ask two people to read the finished page and tell you, in one sentence each, what they think you do. If their sentences don't match your pitch, rewrite the pitch.
